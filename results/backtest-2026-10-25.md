# Backtest skeleton — term 10, content-free

- date: 2026-10-25 · operator: agent (Hermes) + owner review pending
- data: all 5,201 divisions of term 10, per-MP records fetched from the
  official Sejm API (`scripts/fetch_term_dump.py`, resumable; the 251 MB
  JSONL is not committed — regenerate before running)
- protocol: chronological order, warmup 60 divisions, predictions use only
  history strictly earlier than the scored division (no leakage), no titles
  or text anywhere (deliberately content-free to test the harness)
- scoring: CODEBOOK v0.2 — winner exact match (seed rule over tracks),
  per-track yes_share MAE; inactive values ABSENT/PRESENT/VOTE_VALID are
  excluded as non-active votes

## Results (5,079 scored divisions)

| baseline | winner accuracy | yes_share MAE |
|---|---|---|
| always_yes | 0.477 | — |
| club_mean (historical mean share × mean active size) | 0.477 | 0.474 |
| carry_last (previous division's shares) | 0.566 | 0.314 |

Diagnostics: winner is YES in 48.0% of divisions (so always_yes is exactly
a coin flip here); "club's historical majority side predicts today's side"
is correct in 53.1% of 36,497 club-divisions.

## Reading

The harness runs end to end and the metrics are mechanically sound. The
content-free signal is thin: long-run club averages carry almost no
information about today's division (MAE ≈ the naive 0.5), and only
carry-last beats chance, weakly. That is the expected shape — who wins a
division depends on *what is being voted on*, which this layer excludes by
design. So:

1. This is the baseline bar any future model/content method must beat:
   0.566 winner accuracy, 0.314 yes_share MAE.
2. The next layer worth testing is pairwise club affinity (layer 2) and
   then titles as content (layer 3 with text) — both still gradeable.
3. `VOTE_VALID`/`PRESENT` appear in real data (16k records) and are not in
   CODEBOOK v0.2's value list — candidate v0.3 wording fix.

Reproduce: `python scripts/fetch_term_dump.py && python scripts/backtest_skeleton.py divisions.jsonl`
